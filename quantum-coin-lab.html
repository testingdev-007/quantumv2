        Hang on — how can 75% be a ceiling? Open this.
        

          
It sounds like the sort of thing a cleverer pair of agents could beat. They cannot, and that is the remarkable part.

          
In 1964 a physicist called John Bell worked out the maximum win rate for every possible strategy two separated people could agree on in advance — every rule, every code, every clever trick, including ones nobody has thought of yet. The answer came out at exactly 75%. It is a piece of mathematics, not a limit of technology.

          
Entangled particles go past it. Not by cheating and not by sending secret messages — the agents still cannot communicate. Quantum mechanics simply predicts about 85.4%, and when physicists ran the real experiment, that is what they measured.

          
That is why this test matters so much: going above 75% is the proof that entanglement is genuinely real, and it won the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics. You are running the same test.

  • This is the longest activity in the series and it comes first. The Twin Vaults at the end is optional — skipping it costs you nothing but a nice moment.

The beat you are aiming for

    
    
      
  • Intro: the spinning coin, then the boxes. Let someone else press "slap your hand down" — the point lands harder when they do it.
    • 
      
  • Why it works: the noise-cancelling headphones comparison does most of the work. Let them press Next step themselves rather than narrating it.
    • 
      
  • Level 1 — 4 boxes, 1 Search Step. Guaranteed 100%. Not "very likely" — certain. Best opening wow in the series. Name the word sequence out loud while their program is on screen.
    • 
      
  • The Repeat Block. A two-minute stop between Levels 1 and 2. They see six blocks and nine lines of code, drag one block in, and it collapses to one block and four lines. That is the loop taught, and it is the second programming concept of the day. Do not skip it — Level 2 and Level 3 both lean on it.
    • 
      
  • Level 2 — 16 boxes, 3 Search Steps. First chance to use the loop for real, and they can still do it by hand if they prefer. Four times the vault but only three times the steps, and the lede asks them to overshoot deliberately: a fourth step drops them from 96% to 58%. Make sure somebody tries it.
    • 
      
  • Level 3 — 64 boxes, 6 Search Steps, and no readout. This is the teaching level. Two things happen: the meter is switched off so they have to reason rather than optimise against a number, and after two hand-dragged pairs the gold Repeat block unlocks. That is a loop, and the code panel shows it as a real for loop. The readout only appears after they run — then hides again the moment they change anything.
    • 
      
  • Bonus — Twin Vaults. Optional, off the Level 3 screen. No programming in it; it is there because it is astonishing. Skip it if you are short of time, or if the room is flagging.

Questions you will actually get

    
"Is this a real quantum computer?" No — it is an ordinary computer working out what a quantum one would do. What is real is the physics: every number on screen is what a real machine would produce. Say this plainly; people respect it and it makes everything else more credible.

    
"So it is just faster?" No, and this is worth correcting. It is not doing the same work more quickly — it is doing a different kind of work. The Molecule Lab activity makes this point best if they go on to it.

    
"Could this break my bank password?" Not today. Machines are nowhere near big enough. The Cipher Room activity covers why banks are changing their locks anyway.

    
"I don't get the 75% thing." Nor does almost anyone on first pass, which is exactly why it is a bonus rather than part of the lesson. Fall back to: two people in separate rooms, no way to communicate, and no possible arrangement lets them succeed together more than 75% of the time. Entangled equipment manages 85%. Nobody has ever made that feel obvious — it won a Nobel Prize precisely because it is so hard to accept.

    
"Why did the meter disappear in Level 3?" On purpose. With a percentage on screen people optimise against the number without thinking. Take it away and they have to reason, commit to a guess, and find out. Level 3 is also the only place they are asked to call it before running — everywhere else the meter already answers that question, so it would just be an extra click. Let somebody get it wrong once; it is the most useful thing that happens all session.


    
If you only say one thing

    
They wrote a real program today. Not a pretend one, not a simplified one for beginners — the actual algorithm, with the actual maths underneath it. That is worth saying at the end, because most people will not believe it unless you tell them.
